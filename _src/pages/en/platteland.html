Breaking down in the countryside is different

An address isn't enough

On a country lane between two villages there's no house number. Describe a landmark instead: a chapel, a farm, a crossroads, a street sign.

Narrow roads and ditches

Many country lanes are narrow, with ditches on both sides and little room to turn. Describe the road to us. Then we know whether the tow truck can reach the car, or whether we first winch it to a crossroads or a farmyard.

Dark and little traffic

Outside the villages there's no street lighting in the evening. Switch on your hazard lights, put on your high-visibility vest and wait beside or behind the car, off the road. Tractors and lorries only see you late.

An honest arrival time

In the countryside we drive longer than in town. That's why we tell you on the phone when we can realistically be there, and let you know if that changes. You decide.

Getting a car out of a ditch

You pull over for a tractor and a wheel ends up in the ditch. We winch the car back onto the road and check whether it can still be driven.

{{icon:battery-automotive}}

A jump start in the yard

A second car that stood in the yard for weeks won't start any more. We bring a booster right up to the car.

{{icon:route}}

To your own garage

Garages are few and far between in the countryside. We take the car to your usual garage, even if it's in town, for a price agreed in advance.

Frequently asked questions

Do you really go to the smallest villages?

Yes. If a car can get there, so can we. If your village isn't on the list, just call us and tell us where you are.

Is towing more expensive in the countryside?

The price depends on the distance, the destination, what's wrong and the time of day, not on how rural it is. You get a fixed price on the phone before we set off.

My car is on a farm track where the tow truck isn't allowed. What now?

Call us and tell us where you are. We check whether we can winch the car to a paved road, or make arrangements with the owner of the land.
